## 0:45–2:15 · LIVE TERMINAL (`/live`)

*[Click LIVE in the nav.]*

"This is the live maritime picture. Those dots" *[point at the map]* "are ~300 real vessels moving right now, streaming from the Finnish transport agency's open AIS feed, refreshing every 20 seconds. The amber lines sticking out of each dot are their heading vectors — where each ship is pointed. The basemap is real chart geography — OpenStreetMap raster — because a maritime product without recognizable coastlines is useless."

"Right here" *[point at the collision watch table]* "is the part a fleet officer actually cares about. Every pair of ships within 15 nautical miles gets a DCPA and TCPA solve — distance and time to closest point of approach — then a Goodwin-Stanton ship-domain check, and this fuzzy Collision Risk Index. That top row: two vessels 0.13 nautical miles apart, CRI 1.0, domain intrusion flag — that's a real encounter happening as we speak."

"Scroll down — the encounter view draws the actual safety ellipse around the riskiest ship, with CPA extension lines on the chart." *[point]* "And this health strip up top audits our own data: IQR pass rates, z-score outliers, and whether each upstream feed is UP or DOWN. Below: the weather stack — Open-Meteo wind, NOAA point forecasts, and these NDBC buoys are real wave-height readings from instruments in the water. The overlay buttons — QUAKES, MPAS, RADAR, HARBORS — toggle live USGS earthquakes, protected-area polygons, animated weather radar, and OpenStreetMap harbors onto the map. And these SAR chips are actual Sentinel-1 satellite granules over this area."

## 2:15–3:45 · PREDICTION (`/predict`)

*[Click PREDICT.]*

"Now the product itself — the model plus its prediction, laid out. Pick any voyage" *[use the dropdown]* "— say this cargo run. The map tells the whole story: the dark line is where the ship actually went, the dashed red line is the remaining leg, and this teal wedge" *[trace the cone]* "is the **drift cone** — its width is literally the model's P10-to-P90 uncertainty at that point, conformal-calibrated on a holdout. Wider cone = the model is honestly telling you it's less sure."

"These dashed grey lines are the killer feature: **historical analogs** — real previous voyages to the same port with the same ship type. An operator doesn't have to trust a black box; they see the actual trips the prediction is anchored to." *[point at the analog table]* "And the cards on the right: P10, P50, P90 hours — three numbers, not one, because a single ETA is a lie."

## 3:45–5:15 · VOYAGE DOSSIER (`/voyage/{id}`)

*[Click "full dossier →" from the predict page.]*

"From anywhere in the product you can open any voyage's **dossier** — one page with everything about it. The search box up top searches all 8,020 voyages by port, ship type, or month." *[type 'new' in the box, point at results]*

"On one page: the track with drift cone and analogs; the ETA quantiles; the seabed depth profile under the route, sampled from real bathymetry; and the replay" *[click PLAY]* "— a scrubber that walks the ship along its actual track, with its FSM state — transit, maneuvering, drifting — AIS signal drop-out chips showing exactly where the ship went dark, and the fuel-consumption curve from the ITTC-1978 resistance model."

"And at the bottom — the AI analyst. Ask it: 'why will this route drift tomorrow for this ship type?'" *[click the question chip, wait ~15s]* "It answers in four parsed sections — headline, why drift is expected, **reference backing**, what to watch — and the references aren't citations to papers, they're **actual voyage IDs from this dataset**, each one clickable straight into its own dossier. The model underneath is whoever you select here — your OpenCode key first, Groq as the working fallback — and it's forbidden from using numbers that aren't in its context packet."

"Below: the wave-height field with wind barbs from the marine grid, a bathymetric cross-section, and down here" *[scroll]* "the full replay panel with scrubber for any voyage."

### Presenter cheat-sheet

| If asked | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Where do the numbers come from?" | `reports/eval.json`, `drift_summary.json`, `stats.json` — generated by the pipeline; the UI reads those files verbatim. |
| "Is the AI making things up?" | No — the prompt forbids numbers outside the context packet (live weather + model metrics + analog voyages), and each answer names its provider and model. |
| "Commercial use?" | Apache-2.0 code; Open-Meteo's free tier is non-commercial — swap an API key via one env var for production. |
| "Coverage?" | Historical: US waters (NOAA). Live: Baltic (Digitraffic). The pipeline accepts any AIS feed. |
| "Cost to run?" | One laptop. Training is minutes on a laptop GPU; every feed is free. |
