# Voyance Model Card — xgboost-cuda-quantile-eta v1.2.0

**Date:** 2026-10-24 · **Code:** Apache-2.0 · **Contact:** repo issues

## Model details

- **Architecture:** Gradient-boosted regression trees (XGBoost 3.4.1) with quantile
  objectives at α ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9}, trained on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU
  (`tree_method="hist"`, `device="cuda"`). A LightGBM CPU median model is trained
  alongside as a published benchmark, plus a per-type speed-prior baseline.
- **Interval calibration:** Conformalized Quantile Regression (Romano, Patterson,
  Candès — NeurIPS 2019): the P10–P90 band is widened by the (1−α) empirical
  quantile of holdout miscoverage (α = 0.20), computed on a voyage-disjoint
  validation split (MD5 hash of voyage id, 20%).
- **Served via:** FastAPI `POST /v1/predict` returning P10/P50/P90 hours,
  remaining distance, and Beaufort class of the local wind.

## Intended use

Decision support for fleet-operations and maritime-AI teams: ETA-of-arrival
estimates mid-voyage with honest uncertainty, and condition-sliced error analysis
(weather, sea region, vessel type, distance-to-go). Research and open product use.

**Out of scope:** collision avoidance, navigation, naval targeting, any safety-of-life
application. The model predicts remaining voyage time from AIS context; it is not a
certified navigation aid.

## Training data

- AIS: NOAA MarineCadastre daily broadcast points, weeks of Jan 8–14, Apr 8–14,
  Jul 8–14 2024 (CC0). Commercial vessel scope: cargo (AIS 70–79), tanker (80–89),
  passenger (60–69). Tugs, fishing, and other types are excluded: their loitering
  behavior is not an ETA problem and dominated the naive pool.
- Voyage construction: 15 km UN/LOCODE port buffers (20 km spatial clustering of
  adjacent gazetteer entries); a voyage = exit of one port buffer → entry of the
  next (same port counts after ≥ 6 h away); duration 3–240 h; samples ≥ 1 h apart,
  ≥ 8 NM straight-line from the destination, SOG ≥ 0.5 kn.
- Weather join: Open-Meteo archive (ERA5: 10 m wind, gusts, precipitation,
  temperature, humidity) + marine archive (wave height/period/direction, swell),
  0.5° grid, hourly, UTC. Coverage on modeling rows: 100%.
- Counts: see `reports/eval.json` (train/test rows and the voyage-disjoint
  CQR holdout); voyage counts by month in `web/public/data/stats.json`.

## Features (30)

Geographic (lat, lon, great-circle distance-to-go), kinematic (SOG, 6 h median SOG,
distance traveled, elapsed time), vessel (length, draft, type group), weather (wind,
gusts, precipitation, temperature, humidity, wave height/period/direction, swell),
temporal (month, hour cyclic encodings), and three physics-informed ratios
(distance ÷ current speed, ÷ type-median speed, ÷ 6 h median speed). Categoricals
(vessel type group, sea region, departure/arrival port) use deterministic shared
code maps with a reserved "unknown port" bucket.

## Evaluation (July 2024 holdout, real)

All numbers are generated by `pipeline/train.py` into `reports/eval.json` — the
dashboard reads that file directly.

- Benchmark (MAE, hours): XGBoost GPU 4.49 · LightGBM CPU 5.80 · speed prior 5.35.
- P10–P90 empirical coverage after CQR: see `reports/eval.json → coverage_p10_p90`
  (nominal 80%).
- Breakdowns: MAE by Beaufort class, sea region, vessel type group, distance bucket;
  daily MAE series; feature importance (gain).

## Fairness / coverage caveat

Coastal US waters dominate NOAA AIS; small harbor fleets and river systems have
different regimes (the Mississippi's meandering makes straight-line distance a weak
proxy). Condition-sliced metrics expose where the model is weakest — that scorecard
is the product, not an afterthought.

## Drift monitoring

Weekly/monthly slices vs the January feature distribution: Evidently DataDrift
(per-feature PSI/KS) + real model-MAE ratio. Alert rule: PSI > 0.2 on any feature or
MAE ratio > 1.25 vs reference. Served at `GET /v1/drift`; rendered in the dashboard.
